36V vs 48V vs 52V Explained

Voltage is one of two numbers (the other being current, in amps) that together determine how much power a motor can produce. Higher voltage generally means more torque and higher top speed, assuming the controller and motor are rated to handle it.

36V — Common on lighter kick-scooters and entry-level e-bikes. Typically tops out around 15-20 mph.

48V — The most common family for mainstream commuter e-bikes (Rad Power, Aventon, Lectric, Ride1Up). Balances range, speed, and battery weight.

52V — Found on performance-oriented e-bikes (some Super73, Juiced models) where extra torque for hills or cargo hauling matters more than battery size.

The critical rule: these families are not interchangeable just because a battery "fits" physically. A 48V battery on a 36V-rated controller can cause overcurrent damage; a 36V battery on a 48V system will simply underperform. Always match the family your bike was designed for.
